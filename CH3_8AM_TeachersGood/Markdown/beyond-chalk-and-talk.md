# Beyond Chalk and Talk

## The Inspiring Story of Albay's Model Teacher, Elgie Radaza

## Introduction

For Elgie Radaza, education is more than teaching lessons in a classroom—it is about giving people a second chance. Recognized as the "Philippines' Woman of Dedication and Most Innovative Alternative Learning System (ALS) Teacher of the Year" during the 2023 Dangal ng Lahi Awards, Radaza has dedicated her career to helping learners who have been left behind by the traditional education system.

Through her work, she has empowered out-of-school youth, adults, and marginalized learners to believe that their circumstances do not define their future. Her story is one of perseverance, compassion, and an unwavering belief in the transformative power of education.

---

## From Poverty to Purpose

Born into a family of ten children, Radaza grew up in poverty as the daughter of a farmer and a housewife. Despite the financial struggles her family faced, she was determined to change her future through education.

She set a personal goal to become the first member of her family to earn a college degree. Rather than allowing poverty to limit her ambitions, she used it as motivation to work harder and pursue her dreams.

Her personal journey became the foundation of her teaching philosophy. Having experienced hardship herself, she understands the challenges many of her learners face and serves as living proof that determination and education can open doors to a better life.

---

## Giving Learners a Second Chance

As an Alternative Learning System teacher, Radaza works with students who have dropped out of formal schooling for various reasons, including poverty, family responsibilities, and personal challenges.

She believes that every learner deserves an opportunity to continue their education regardless of their circumstances.

One of her former students, Nicole Shane Mendaza, was forced to leave school because of teenage pregnancy. Through Radaza's guidance and encouragement, Nicole regained confidence and eventually completed her studies.

Nicole described her teacher as a second parent who provided support, advice, and motivation during one of the most difficult periods of her life. More than academic lessons, Radaza taught her to believe in herself and trust her ability to succeed.

---

## Teaching Beyond the Classroom

For Radaza, teaching is not simply about delivering information. She believes that the influence a teacher has on a student's life often matters more than the lessons taught in class.

Throughout her 14 years of service as an ALS teacher, she has mentored countless out-of-school children, youth, and adults. While she may no longer remember every learner she has helped, she remembers the stories and transformations that demonstrate the impact of education.

One learner who remains especially memorable was a 64-year-old grandmother who enrolled in the ALS program despite her age. The woman diligently completed her learning modules and remained eager to learn.

Radaza admired her determination and dedication. She explained that the elderly learner reminded her of her own mother, who deeply valued education despite only completing Grade 4.

This experience reinforced her belief that learning has no age limit and that education remains a lifelong journey.

---

## A Role Model at Home and at Work

Radaza's influence extends beyond her students.

At home, she is a mother of five and became the family's primary breadwinner after her husband was involved in a motorcycle accident in 2017. Despite the challenges of balancing work and family responsibilities, she continued to support both her loved ones and her students.

Her son describes her as a "wonder woman" because of her ability to remain positive, calm, and resilient even during difficult times.

Former students and fellow educators also admire her dedication. One former mentee who later became a teacher herself credits Radaza's commitment to excellence and her ability to adapt to changing educational demands.

Other colleagues point to her passion for service and her tireless efforts to meet the needs of every learner.

---

## Recognition and Achievement

Over the years, Radaza has received numerous awards recognizing her contributions to education.

In 2022, she became a finalist for the Most Inspiring ALS Teacher during the Albay Recognition of Unconditional and Genuine Action for Nobility (ARUGAN) Awards. In 2023, she received national recognition through the prestigious Dangal ng Lahi Awards.

While these achievements brought honour to her profession, Radaza believes that awards are not the true measure of success.

For her, the greatest accomplishment is seeing her students overcome obstacles, complete their education, and achieve their dreams.

---

## Lessons from Elgie Radaza's Story

Radaza's journey demonstrates how education can transform lives and create opportunities for those who need them most.

### Key Takeaways

- Education can provide a second chance for individuals facing difficult circumstances.
- Poverty does not determine a person's future.
- Teachers can have a lifelong impact through encouragement and mentorship.
- Learning is possible at any age.
- Success is often measured by the lives we help improve, not by the awards we receive.
- Determination and self-belief are essential for overcoming challenges.

---

## Conclusion

Elgie Radaza's story is a powerful example of how one dedicated teacher can influence countless lives. From overcoming poverty herself to helping others find hope through education, she has become a symbol of resilience, compassion, and service.

Her belief in second chances continues to inspire learners of all ages, proving that education is not merely about acquiring knowledge—it is about creating opportunities, building confidence, and empowering people to shape their own futures.

For Radaza, the greatest reward is not recognition or awards, but watching her students succeed and knowing that she played a small part in helping them reach their dreams.
